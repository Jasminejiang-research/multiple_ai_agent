# Porter Five Forces Framework

## Purpose

Porter's Five Forces assesses the structural pressures that influence
competition and profit potential in a clearly defined industry. It evaluates
industry economics, not whether one company is inherently "good" or "bad."

## Scope First

Record:

- industry and value-chain boundary;
- customer segment and geography;
- present period and relevant future horizon;
- product category and substitutes included;
- evidence date, because industry structure changes.

## 1. Rivalry Among Existing Competitors

Assess competitor concentration, growth, differentiation, switching costs,
fixed costs, capacity, exit barriers, and the frequency of price competition.

**Questions:** How intensely do firms compete? Is value captured through price,
differentiation, distribution, or scale?

## 2. Threat of New Entrants

Assess capital needs, scale economies, network effects, brand, distribution
access, regulation, proprietary capability, switching costs, and likely
incumbent response.

**Questions:** What must a credible entrant invest or obtain? How durable are
the barriers?

## 3. Bargaining Power of Buyers

Assess buyer concentration, purchase size, price sensitivity, switching
costs, information access, alternatives, procurement process, and ability to
integrate backward.

**Questions:** Who controls the purchasing decision? Can buyers force lower
prices or higher service levels?

## 4. Bargaining Power of Suppliers

Assess supplier concentration, input uniqueness, switching cost, substitutes,
labor or platform dependencies, forward-integration risk, and the importance
of the industry to suppliers.

**Questions:** Which scarce inputs can capture margin or constrain growth?

## 5. Threat of Substitutes

Identify different products, workflows, or non-consumption that solve the same
customer job. Assess relative price-performance, switching friction, customer
habit, and emerging technology.

**Questions:** What else can deliver the outcome, and why would customers
switch?

## Synthesis

Conclude with:

1. the two forces most likely to shape profitability;
2. how the forces may change over the chosen horizon;
3. which positions or capabilities could reduce pressure;
4. which assumptions require research or testing.

## Quality Checks

- The industry boundary is explicit and consistently applied.
- Competitors and substitutes are distinguished.
- A list of competitor names is not used as a rivalry analysis.
- Barriers to entry are supported by evidence rather than asserted.
- Each rating explains its drivers and business implication.
- The conclusion acknowledges uncertainty and structural change.
