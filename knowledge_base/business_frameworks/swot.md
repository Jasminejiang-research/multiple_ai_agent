# SWOT Analysis Framework

## Purpose

SWOT organizes internal strengths and weaknesses alongside external
opportunities and threats. It is a synthesis tool, not a substitute for
customer, competitor, market, or operational evidence.

## When to Use

- framing an initial strategic position;
- comparing the organization's capabilities with market conditions;
- identifying issues that require validation before planning;
- translating evidence into a short list of strategic priorities.

## Inputs

- customer and stakeholder research;
- product, team, financial, and operating data;
- competitor and substitute analysis;
- market, technology, regulatory, and macroeconomic evidence.

## The Four Categories

| Category | Orientation | Guiding question | Example evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strengths | Internal, helpful | What verified capability or asset creates an advantage? | Retention, proprietary access, expertise, distribution |
| Weaknesses | Internal, harmful | What internal limitation reduces the chance of success? | Skill gap, concentration risk, poor unit economics |
| Opportunities | External, helpful | What external change or unmet need could be captured? | Underserved segment, regulation, channel shift |
| Threats | External, harmful | What external force could undermine the plan? | New entrant, substitution, price pressure, policy change |

## Method

1. Define the decision, market, geography, and time horizon.
2. Gather evidence before brainstorming labels.
3. Classify each factor as internal or external and helpful or harmful.
4. Write factors specifically enough to support an action.
5. Attach a `source_id`, metric, or explicit assumption to every item.
6. Rank factors by evidence confidence and strategic impact.
7. Combine factors into testable strategic options.

## From Inventory to Strategy

- **SO:** use a strength to capture an opportunity.
- **WO:** address a weakness so an opportunity becomes reachable.
- **ST:** use a strength to reduce exposure to a threat.
- **WT:** reduce a weakness and avoid or contain a threat.

| Pair | Strategic response | Owner | Validation step | Success signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `[S1 + O2]` | `[Action]` | `[Role]` | `[Experiment]` | `[Metric]` |

## Quality Checks

- Factors are specific, non-duplicative, and relevant to the defined scope.
- Internal factors are not confused with market conditions.
- Positive aspirations are not presented as current strengths.
- Opportunities are external conditions, not proposed actions.
- Each priority has evidence, an owner, and a validation step.
- The output identifies trade-offs instead of treating every item as urgent.

## Common Failure Modes

- producing four unranked brainstorming lists;
- using vague labels such as "good team" or "large market";
- presenting the same issue in multiple quadrants;
- treating unsupported beliefs as facts;
- stopping at the matrix without forming strategic actions.
